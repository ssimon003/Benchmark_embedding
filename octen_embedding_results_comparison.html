Octen embedding benchmark comparison

Three Octen embedding sizes were tested on 6,993 Dutch and English survey answers. The 8B model wins on quality, the 4B model is very close while running much faster, and the 0.6B model is the speed option with a clear quality drop.

Plain-English Result

8B is the quality winner. It is best on every major quality metric: `domain_score`, `domain_score_200`, `MAP@10`, `MAP@200`, cross-language retrieval, topic-query accuracy, linear probing, and clustering.
4B is the practical tradeoff. It keeps about 98% of the 8B top-10 domain score and about 97% of the 8B top-200 domain score, while finishing the embedding run about 38% faster.
0.6B is much faster but weaker. It runs in 23.77 seconds, but loses a lot of quality, especially on cross-language retrieval and query-to-topic matching.
For many answers, look at `domain_score_200`. Since your embedding model will search across a large pool, the `@200` metrics are the better stress test. The ranking remains 8B, then 4B, then 0.6B.

What The Differences Mean

Top-10 retrieval

`MAP@10` and `NDCG@10` check whether the first page of retrieved answers is clean. The 8B model is best, but 4B is close: 0.614 vs 0.605 on `MAP@10`.

Top-200 retrieval

`MAP@200` is harder because it checks a much deeper list. Scores are lower, but the ranking is stable: 8B at 0.326, 4B at 0.314, 0.6B at 0.262.

Speed

8B takes 154.5 seconds, 4B takes 95.39 seconds, and 0.6B takes 23.77 seconds. The 4B model is the strongest speed-quality compromise.

Recommendation: use Octen 8B when retrieval quality matters most. Use Octen 4B when you want almost the same ranking behavior with much faster embedding. Use Octen 0.6B only when speed or hardware limits are the main concern.

Why `@200` Drops But `MRR` Does Not

`MAP@200` and `NDCG@200` drop because they judge a much larger part of the ranked list. It is harder to keep 200 retrieved answers clean than 10. `MRR@200` stays almost the same as `MRR@10` because MRR only asks where the first correct same-topic answer appears. If the first correct answer is already in the top 10, increasing the cutoff to 200 barely changes MRR.

Chance topic accuracy for seven balanced topics is roughly 0.143, so all three models are well above chance. The question is not whether they work, but how much quality you want to pay for in speed and model size.
